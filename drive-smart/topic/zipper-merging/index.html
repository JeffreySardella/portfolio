Zipper Merging

⚡ Quick Tips

  • Merge like a zipper — one from each lane, alternating
  • Don't merge early — use the full lane and merge at the point
  • Speed up or slow down to match the lane you're merging into — never merge at a speed difference
  • See someone merging? Ease off the gas slightly and let them in

It’s Not a Race, It’s a Zipper

Merge like a zipper — one from each lane, alternating

▼

Most people treat merging like a competition. They speed up to “win” or slam their brakes because they refuse to let anyone in. A zipper merge is exactly what it sounds like — one car from the left, one from the right, alternating. It’s the fastest way to move two lanes of traffic into one. Studies show it reduces backup length by up to 40% compared to the “merge early and hope for the best” approach. Stop competing. Start alternating.

Use the Full Merge Lane

Don't merge early — use the full lane and merge at the point

▼

You’ve seen it: a lane ends in half a mile, and people start merging immediately, leaving an entire empty lane while one lane backs up for a mile. This is slower for everyone. The merge lane exists for a reason — drive in it until the merge point, then alternate. Merging early doesn’t make you polite, it makes the backup longer. Use the full lane and merge at the end where drivers can take turns predictably.

If You’re Merging: Match Their Speed

Speed up or slow down to match the lane you're merging into — never merge at a speed difference

▼

The most dangerous thing you can do during a merge is enter a lane going significantly faster or slower than the traffic in it. As the merging driver, your job is to adjust your speed to match the flow of the lane you’re entering. Use the merge lane to accelerate or decelerate until you’re moving at the same pace. A smooth merge is one where the other lane barely notices you joined.

If You’re in the Through Lane: Make a Gap

See someone merging? Ease off the gas slightly and let them in

▼

Merging is a two-person job. If you’re in the through lane and you see cars needing to merge, don’t speed up to close the gap. Ease off the gas a little, create space, and let one car in. You lose maybe 2 seconds. The alternative — forcing them to brake and merge from a near-stop — creates a chain reaction that slows everyone behind you for minutes. Making a gap isn’t charity, it’s self-interest. Smooth merges mean you get home faster too.
