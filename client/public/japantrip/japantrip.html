💡 First Night Rules
  • Get cash from 7-Eleven ATM tonight, not tomorrow when you're rushing
  • Download Google Maps offline for Japan before you land
  • Set Suica on Apple/Google Pay — faster than tapping physical card
  • Jet lag rule: stay up until 9:30 PM local time, no matter what
  • Don't eat a big meal — your stomach is confused. Matsuya gyudon (~¥500) or convenience store is perfect
💡 Ikebukuro For Later in the Trip
  • Gashapon Department Store (Sunshine City) — world's largest gachapon, 3,000–4,000 machines, ¥300–400/capsule
  • Namco Nanja Town (Sunshine City B2) — indoor theme park, Gyoza Stadium with regional gyoza from all over Japan
  • Sunshine 60 Observatory (251m) — less crowded than Skytree, panoramic views
  • Animate Ikebukuro — 8 floors of anime merch, the biggest in Tokyo
  • Otome Road — east of Sunshine City, all the doujin/BL/manga specialty shops
🔄 If Flight is Delayed

Pocket WiFi desks often close by 10 PM. If you arrive very late, use eSIM data that night and pick up WiFi first thing tomorrow morning at Ikebukuro Station service counter. Don't panic — your hotel has WiFi.

🔄 If Jet Lag Hits Tomorrow

Tomorrow (May 28) is Harajuku/Shibuya — flexible day. Push it back an hour if needed. The city doesn't close. Meiji Shrine is a gentle first stop that works any time of day.

➕ If You Have Energy Tonight

Walk Sunshine City (open until 8 PM) for a quick orientation to Ikebukuro. Gashapon Department Store closes at 8 PM — quick first gachapon fix. Stay local. Don't go to Shibuya tonight.

🍜 Tonight: Keep It Simple
  • Matsuya — gyudon beef bowl ~¥500, open 24 hrs, walk-in, no Japanese needed (ticket machine)
  • Sushiro — conveyor sushi near Ikebukuro Station, reasonable and filling
  • 7-Eleven — onigiri + hot coffee + egg salad sandwich is an underrated airport recovery meal
  • Skip alcohol tonight. Your body is confused enough.

🌸 Harajuku & Shibuya

May 28 Tokyo
▸

10:30 AMTrain to Harajuku (Yamanote Line).

12:15 PMWalk Omotesando Ave → Takeshita Street. Crepes, Cotton Candy, wild fashion.
1:30 PMLunch near Harajuku Station.
2:30 PMTrain to Shibuya (2 min on Yamanote).
3 PMShibuya Scramble Crossing. Ground level first. Then aerial view: Starbucks Tsutaya (free, window seats) or Shibuya Sky (~¥2,000). Book Shibuya Sky →
4 PMShibuya: Center-gai, Don Quijote, Tower Records (look for J-pop floor).
6:30 PMDinner in Shibuya area.
8 PMNonbei Yokocho — tiny lantern-lit bar alley behind Tower Records. Best 7–10 PM.

💡 Shibuya Hidden
  • Nonbei Yokocho — behind Tower Records, narrow alley of tiny bars. Best 7–10 PM. Say "ippon kudasai" (one glass please)
  • Daikanyama — 15 min walk south. Canal walks, boutiques, % Arabica coffee queue
  • Nakameguro — 5 more min from Daikanyama. Best riverside canal in Tokyo, great for drinks
  • Shimokitazawa — 10 min from Shibuya on Keio Inokashira Line. Vintage clothing capital of Tokyo, hundreds of second-hand shops, live music bars
  • Tomigaya — just north of Yoyogi Park. Called "Shibuya for adults" by locals. Scandinavian coffee shops, no tourists
💡 Meiji Shrine Tips
  • Arrive before noon to avoid tour groups
  • The inner garden (Gyoen) costs ¥500 but has the iris garden (in bloom in June!)
  • Wine barrels and sake barrels at the entrance are real gifts to the shrine — don't miss reading the labels
🔄 If It's Raining

Meiji Shrine is beautiful in light rain. Bring umbrella. Omotesando has covered shopping. Shibuya Scramble is still excellent in rain — more atmospheric. Shibuya Sky is outdoors though — skip if heavy rain, get refund online (up to 24h before).

🔄 If Jet Lag Hits and You Need a Slow Day

Do just Harajuku in the morning. Spend the afternoon walking Omotesando's designer boutiques and side streets (no rushing required). Skip Shibuya for another day — you'll pass through it many times on the Yamanote loop.

➕ If You Have Extra Time — Shimokitazawa

From Shibuya, take the Keio Inokashira Line (2 stops, ~5 min, ¥140) to Shimokitazawa. The streets are too narrow for cars. Spend 2 hours: Flamingo vintage shop, Bonus Track courtyard (books + bars + pop-ups), any random record shop. Dinner at an izakaya here instead of Shibuya — more local, cheaper.

➕ If You Want Luxury Shopping

Omotesando Hills mall is on the main drag — Prada, Louis Vuitton, Issey Miyake. The architecture (Tadao Ando) is worth seeing even if you don't buy. Also: the side streets between Omotesando and Harajuku have the best emerging Japanese designers.

🍜 Harajuku / Shibuya Eats
  • Harajuku Crepes — Takeshita Street stalls. Angel Heart or Marion Crepes. Strawberry + cream + custard.
  • Gyukatsu Motomura (Shibuya) — beef cutlet you cook yourself on a mini grill. Queue outside worth it. →
  • Ichiran Ramen — solo booth ramen, customize spice + richness + noodle. English menu. →
  • Fuunji (Shinjuku, evening) — famous tsukemen dipping ramen. Be prepared to queue 20–40 min.

Tickets →
1 PMLunch at Solamachi (base mall) — huge food hall with options for everyone.
2 PMTrain to Shinjuku (~35 min).
2:30 PMTokyo Metropolitan Government Building (west exit of Shinjuku) — FREE observation deck. Open until 10:30 PM. Best free view in Tokyo.
4 PMShinjuku: Kabukicho neon walk, Omoide Yokocho exploration.
6–9 PMOmoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) — yakitori skewers under the tracks. Arrive 6 PM before it fills. Order: momo, tsukune, negima. Say "shio" (salt) or "tare" (sweet soy).
9 PMGolden Gai (~5-min walk) — 200 tiny bars across 6 alleys. Some have ¥500–1,000 cover — ask before sitting. Best 9 PM–1 AM.
10 PMBack to Ikebukuro. IN BED by 11 PM — GO Fest tomorrow.
💡 Senso-ji Tips
  • Fortune sticks (omikuji) ¥100 — if you get a bad fortune, tie it to the rack and leave it there
  • The incense smoke (draftsman) — wave it over yourself for health and good luck
  • Best photo angle: stand at the far end of Nakamise looking back at the Thunder Gate
  • Nakamise shops open around 10 AM — don't rush to buy at 8 AM, just walk through
  • Try ningyo-yaki (doll-shaped cakes, ¥200) and kaminari okoshi (rice crackers)
💡 Golden Gai Tips
  • Bars hold 5–10 people. If a bar is full, just move on — there are 200
  • Ask "cover charge arimasu ka?" (is there a cover?) before sitting
  • Most bars have a theme: jazz, anime, horror, cats, etc. Pick by vibe
  • Dragonfly bar is the most tourist-friendly for first-timers if you're nervous
  • After Golden Gai: walk through Kabukicho for the neon spectacle, just don't engage with touts
🔄 If Skytree Tickets are Sold Out

Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building is free and nearly as high — go there instead. Or: Sunshine 60 in Ikebukuro (close to hotel, ¥700). Or Shibuya Sky if you skipped it yesterday.

🔄 If Rain

Senso-ji is atmospheric in rain. Kappabashi is entirely covered. Solamachi at Skytree base is a massive indoor mall. Golden Gai is indoors. This is actually a great rainy day plan as-is.

➕ Yanaka — Old Tokyo (Add Between Asakusa and Skytree)

From Asakusa, walk north 25 min or take Ginza Line 2 stops to Ueno then walk to Yanaka. This is the most intact Edo-era neighborhood in Tokyo — survived both the 1923 earthquake and WWII bombing. Yanaka Ginza shopping street, 70+ temples, Nezu Shrine (mini Fushimi Inari, rarely crowded). Kayaba Coffee for a century-old kissaten. Look for the cats — Yanaka is known as Tokyo's Cat Town.

➕ Kagurazaka (Evening Alternative to Shinjuku)

Former geisha district between Shinjuku and Iidabashi. The stone alleys (yokocho) behind the main street hide old ryotei restaurants, jazz bars, and tiny French cafés. Akagi Jinja shrine has a stunningly modern Kengo Kuma design. Best for a slower evening walk.

🍜 Asakusa / Shinjuku Eats
  • Kaminari Okoshi — rice crackers from stalls on Nakamise, made right there
  • Omoide Yokocho — yakitori skewers ¥200–400 each. Order 5–8 sticks to fill up
  • Ichiran Ramen near Shinjuku — solo booth, fully customizable, excellent after Golden Gai
  • Tsunahachi (Shinjuku) — tempura set since 1923, lunch sets from ¥1,500

~5 PMGO Fest winds down. Dinner in Odaiba area.

💡 GO Fest Strategy
  • Check habitat rotation schedule in-app the night before. Know which habitats run when.
  • Agree on a meet-up spot if you split up — Unicorn Gundam plaza is easy landmark
  • Bring a physical portable charger AND a USB-C cable. Your phone will die.
  • The event area is large — wear comfortable shoes, bring water
  • Check Discord/Reddit for raid trains (organized groups hopping from raid to raid)
💡 Odaiba While You're There
  • Unicorn Gundam Statue (DiverCity) — transforms at 11 AM, 1 PM, 3 PM, 5 PM. Night shows every 30 min 7–9:30 PM. FREE.
  • Doraemon Times Square — outside DiverCity, plays the theme hourly 10 AM–11 PM
  • Small Worlds Tokyo — world's largest indoor miniature theme park, near Kokusai-tenjijo Station. Worth seeing if you finish early.
  • Tokyo Statue of Liberty + Rainbow Bridge — on the Odaiba waterfront, great photo backdrop
  • Note: teamLab Planets is in TOYOSU (not Odaiba) — different stop on the Yurikamome
🔄 If Weather is Bad

GO Fest is outdoors but continues in rain. Bring a poncho or compact umbrella. The indoor Aqua City and DiverCity malls are covered. You can still catch Pokémon under awnings. The event does NOT cancel for rain.

🔄 If Phone Dies Mid-Day

DiverCity 3F has a paid charging station. Aqua City has a coin locker area with USB ports. Keep the portable battery charged from the night before — this is the highest-stakes day for battery.

➕ teamLab Planets — Toyosu (Evening Add-On)

If you have tickets and energy after GO Fest: Yurikamome Line from Odaiba-Kaihinkoen to Shin-Toyosu (8 min). teamLab Planets is a barefoot water walk experience — different from Borderless, more focused, takes ~90 min. Book in advance. Plain/dark clothes for best photos.

➕ Go-Kart Instead of Kart + Dinner

City Circuit Tokyo Bay: EV karts, no license needed, 2-min from Tokyo Teleport. Street Kart through Shibuya requires IDP (license) and is more of a spectacle — only do if you got the IDP from AAA.

🍜 Odaiba Eats
  • Aqua City Ramen Street — basement level, 8+ regional ramen styles. Tsukemen, tonkotsu, miso. Best ramen variety in one spot.
  • DiverCity Food Court — large, quick, good variety. Don't miss the Ichiran branch here.
  • Decks Tokyo Beach mall — has a Hong Kong food street theme area on the upper floors, kitschy but fun

May 31 Tokyo
▸

10:30 AMAkihabara route: Walk Chuo-dori for orientation → Yodobashi Akiba (electronics) → Radio Kaikan (10 floors, figures/models/vintage) → Super Potato (1-11-2 Sotokanda, 3F) — retro games, playable → Mandarake (3-11-12 Sotokanda, 8 floors of used manga + figures) → GiGO arcades.

5 PMteamLab Borderless. ~¥3,800+. Allow 2–2.5 hrs. Wear plain/dark clothes for photos. Location: Azabudai Hills. Tickets →
7:30 PMDinner near teamLab or back to Akihabara for late arcades (open midnight+).
💡 Arcade Deep Tips
  • Watch others play UFO catcher before spending — see how the crane is positioned
  • Ask staff "okuroi onegaishimasu" — they can reposition prizes, some will do it
  • Spend max ¥500–1,000 per machine before moving on
  • Try: Taiko no Tatsujin (taiko drums), maimai DX, CHUNITHM — all iconic Japanese rhythm games
  • Purikura photo booths are on upper floors of most Round1 locations — great group souvenir
💡 teamLab Borderless Tips
  • Plain/dark clothes — you want the art to project onto you, not your loud t-shirt
  • No shoes in several rooms — wear slip-on shoes or sandals
  • The lamp forest room and floating flowers room are the best for photos
  • Arrive 10 min before your slot — they're strict
  • Takes 2–2.5 hrs to see everything. Don't rush the first room.
🔄 If teamLab is Sold Out

teamLab Planets in Toyosu is a different (and slightly smaller) experience — often has more availability. Book same-day on the website. Alternatively, spend the evening in Akihabara doing arcades until midnight — equally memorable.

➕ Koenji (Alternative If Already Done Akihabara)

If you've seen Akihabara enough: Koenji is Tokyo's underground music/punk scene. Central Line from Akihabara, 15 min. Browse vintage shops on Etoile Street, grab vinyl at Record BOY, have dinner at an izakaya under the tracks. Completely different energy from Akihabara — more local, less tourist.

➕ Nakano Broadway (Akihabara Alternative)

Nakano Broadway is 1 stop west of Shinjuku on the Chuo Line. Less tourists than Akihabara but huge collection of rare figures, vintage games, Mandarake has multiple stores here. Better for rare/vintage finds than current merchandise.

🍜 Akihabara / Roppongi Eats
  • AKB48 Café — touristy, but you're already in Akihabara, no judgment
  • Gonpachi Nishi-Azabu — the "Kill Bill" restaurant near Roppongi. Great yakitori. →
  • Random Akihabara izakaya under the train tracks — the stretch of bars underneath the elevated railway between Akihabara and Ochanomizu is excellent late-night. Just walk in.

🍜 Jun 1 Eats

  • Return to Ikebukuro for a good carb-loading dinner before Disney: Sushiro conveyor sushi or local ramen
  • Pack snacks and a real water bottle — theme park prices are steep
DAY 7

🌿 Kamakura Day Trip

Jun 2 Tokyo
▸
8:30 AMDepart Ikebukuro. Shonan Shinjuku Line direct to Kamakura (~60 min, ~¥1,000). Get off at Kita-Kamakura (one stop early) for the best approach.
9:30 AMHase-dera Temple. Hydrangeas at peak bloom in June — 2,500 plants, 40+ varieties. ⚠️ Arrive before 10 AM — timed entry for the hydrangea path fills by 11 AM. ¥400. Site →
11 AMGreat Buddha (Kotoku-in). 13m bronze. ¥300. Go inside for ¥50 extra. Site →
12 PMLunch on Komachi-dori street. Try shirasudon — whitebait rice bowl, Kamakura's local specialty.
1:30 PMZeniarai Benzaiten. FREE. Hidden cave shrine — wash money for good fortune. Walk through the narrow tunnel. Atmospheric even if you don't believe it.
3 PMOptional: Enoshima Island by Enoden train (20 min). Sea caves, lighthouse, ocean views. Enoshima-Kamakura Freepass: ¥1,640 from Shinjuku (includes unlimited Enoden + round trip).
5:30 PMReturn to Ikebukuro. In bed by 10 PM — DisneySea is a 7 AM departure tomorrow.
💡 Kamakura Hidden Gems
  • Meigetsu-in Temple (Kita-Kamakura) — famous round window that frames the garden. Peak hydrangea bloom 2nd week of June. ¥500. Much less crowded than Hase-dera.
  • Sasuke Inari Shrine — a mini Fushimi Inari hidden in the hills. Almost no tourists. Free.
  • Hokokuji Temple — small bamboo grove (quieter than Arashiyama). ¥400, or ¥1,000 with matcha in the grove. The matcha version is worth it.
  • Daibutsu Hiking Trail (from Kita-Kamakura) — 3km through wooded hills, leads to Zeniarai Benzaiten and then the Great Buddha. Takes ~2 hrs total.
💡 Getting Around Kamakura
  • Enoden tram is the scenic coastal way to get between Kamakura and Enoshima — worth taking even without the freepass
  • Hase-dera area and Great Buddha are 20-min walk from Kamakura Station or 5-min on Enoden
  • The Kita-Kamakura approach: get off one stop early and walk 30 min south through quiet residential/temple streets to Kamakura Station
🔄 Option B: Harry Potter Studio Tour

Warner Bros. Studio Tour Tokyo is 20 min from Ikebukuro on the Seibu Ikebukuro Line (Toshimaen Station). No walk-in tickets — must book ahead. Adults ~¥7,000. 4–5 hrs. Good rainy day option since it's indoors. Book →

🔄 If Hase-dera Hydrangea Queue is Too Long

Walk to Meigetsu-in instead — it's in Kita-Kamakura and has the famous round window plus hydrangeas. Far fewer people. Then Sasuke Inari for the quiet woodland shrine feeling. You get the same hydrangea season experience without the 90-min wait.

➕ Enoshima Island

20 min from Kamakura on the Enoden tram. An island connected by a bridge with sea caves (Iwaya Caves, ¥500), a lighthouse (¥360), and ocean views. Best for afternoon after you've done Hase-dera and the Great Buddha. The seafood on the island bridge approach is excellent — grilled octopus, raw clams.

🍜 Kamakura Eats
  • Shirasudon — baby whitebait over rice. Kamakura's local food. Tons of spots on Komachi-dori.
  • Matcha soft serve — every other shop on Komachi-dori. Get one.
  • Warashibe — wagashi (traditional Japanese sweets) shop on Komachi-dori. Seasonal treats.
  • Mizuya Chaya — thatched-roof restaurant near Kasuga Taisha in Nara, but also: look for equivalent rustic spots near Hase-dera with great udon.

💡 DisneySea Strategy
  • Open the Tokyo Disney Resort app before entering — have your account ready
  • Fantasy Springs Priority Access opens when the park does — grab it the MOMENT gates open
  • Journey to the Center of the Earth has no Skip equivalent — go at opening or accept a long wait
  • June is rainy season — bring a compact poncho or umbrella. Many rides are indoors anyway.
  • The park food is legitimately good. Mickey-shaped churros, Mediterranean Harbor seafood, Duffy merchandise everywhere.
🔄 If Fantasy Springs Wait Times are Insane

Do the port-of-call areas in the morning while crowds concentrate in Fantasy Springs. Come back to Fantasy Springs in the evening when it thins. The 3 Fantasy Springs rides are great but the park around them is also excellent — don't let the queue FOMO ruin the day.

➕ DisneySea Merchandise Notes

DisneySea-exclusive merchandise is only sold here — Duffy and Friends (a huge thing in Japan, even if you don't know them), port-specific food items, JDM Disney items not available in the US. Budget ¥3,000–8,000 for merch if you care about it. The shops at the gate close after the park — buy before 8 PM.

🍜 DisneySea Eats
  • Vulcania (Lost River Delta) — Portuguese-inspired hot meals, quieter restaurant
  • Miguel's El Dorado Cantina — nachos and tacos, quick service in American Waterfront
  • Turtle Talk snack cart — grab gyoza, shrimp cocktails, churros while walking
  • Tip: Pack a couple of onigiri or snacks in your bag — park food is ¥800–1,800 per item

🗻 Travel to Kawaguchiko

Jun 5 Fuji

💡 Deer Tips
  • The deer near Todai-ji are the pushiest — everyone feeds them there. For calmer interactions, feed them away from the temple.
  • Bow slowly to a deer — they'll bow back. Then give them the cracker.
  • If surrounded, show empty hands spread wide — the deer understand this means "no more food"
  • Bags, maps, and loose clothing get grabbed — keep food sealed and bags zipped
  • Harming the deer was once punishable by death. They know it. Act accordingly.

📋 Essentials

Everything you need to know before leaving the hotel. Reference sections for trains, money, food, nightlife, and more.

🚆 Trains & Suica

  • Load Suica on Apple/Google Pay before leaving home — tap your phone, way faster than a physical card
  • Suica works on virtually all trains, buses, and convenience stores across Japan
  • JR Pass: only worth it if doing the Shinkansen multiple times. For your itinerary: Tokyo→Hiroshima→Tokyo is likely worth it. Calculate before buying.
  • IC card tapping: tap IN and tap OUT every time. Forgetting to tap out = overcharge.
  • Last train: most lines stop ~midnight. Yamanote runs until 1 AM on weekends. Know this before planning late nights.
  • Google Maps (offline) for Japan is excellent for train routing. Download the Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka/Hiroshima areas.
  • Shinkansen seat reservations: FREE with JR Pass, ~¥1,000–3,000 without. Always book ahead for long routes.

💳 Money & ATMs

  • 7-Eleven ATMs are the most reliable for foreign cards. Japan Post ATMs (in post offices) are also reliable.
  • Keep ¥10,000–20,000 cash on you at all times. Many smaller restaurants and shops are cash-only.
  • Tax-free shopping: spend ¥5,000+ at a single store with your passport for 10% tax exemption. Electronics stores, department stores, some pharmacies.
  • Coins matter: ¥500 = ~$3.50. ¥100 = ~$0.70. Vending machines, lockers, smaller purchases all use coins.
  • Don't tip. Ever. It's rude and confusing to service staff.
  • Airport duty-free: whisky (Hibiki, Nikka, Yamazaki), matcha KitKat variety boxes, Shiroi Koibito cookies.

🍜 Food Guide

  • Ramen types: tonkotsu (pork, rich), shoyu (soy, lighter), miso (Hokkaido style), shio (salt, clearest). Don't drink the soup at the end — it's too salty.
  • Conveyor sushi (kaiten): plates are ~¥130–330. Touch the screen at your seat to order specific items. Cheaper than you think.
  • Izakaya order: order drinks first, then food slowly throughout the evening. Standard is: beer → food → another round → more food → highball to finish.
  • Set meals (teishoku): lunch specials are extraordinary value. Many restaurants do ¥800–1,200 sets with rice, miso soup, pickles, and the main dish.
  • Ticket machines: most ramen shops use vending machine ordering. Pick your dish (often has photos), take the ticket to your seat or hand to staff.
  • Convenience store food (konbini) is genuinely good: fresh onigiri, egg salad sandwiches, hot nikuman (meat buns), decent fried chicken.

🌙 Nightlife

  • Golden Gai (Shinjuku): 6 alleys, ~200 bars, each holds 5–10 people. Ask about cover charge before sitting. Best 9 PM–2 AM.
  • Omoide Yokocho (Shinjuku): yakitori under the tracks. Arrive 6 PM. Very cramped, very good.
  • Nonbei Yokocho (Shibuya): behind Tower Records. Lantern-lit tiny bar alley. Slightly more upscale than Golden Gai.
  • Ura Namba (Osaka): standing bars 3 blocks south of Dotonbori. Where locals actually drink.
  • Pontocho (Kyoto): 500m lantern alley with izakaya and bars. Best after 6 PM when restaurants open for dinner.
  • Last train midnight. Taxi home = very expensive. Know your line's last departure.

🕹️ Arcades

  • UFO catchers (crane games): Watch before you play. ¥100–200 per play. Budget ¥500–1,000 max per machine then move on.
  • "Okuroi onegaishimasu" — ask staff to reposition the prize. Some will do it.
  • Rhythm games: Taiko no Tatsujin (taiko drums), maimai DX (touchscreen circle), CHUNITHM (air sensor). All have English interfaces.
  • Purikura (photo sticker booths): upper floors of most arcades. Great group photo souvenir. Skin-smoothing filters are automatic and intense.
  • GiGO arcades in Akihabara are the best for Pokémon/anime UFO catchers.
  • Round1 entertainment centers are everywhere — have everything plus bowling.
  • Arcades stay open until midnight or 2 AM.

🛁 Onsen Guide

  • Most onsen prohibit tattoos — always check before going
  • Tattoo-friendly in Kawaguchiko: Ooike Hotel (best, Fuji view rotenburo), Hotel Koryu, Rakuyu
  • Wash COMPLETELY at the shower stations before entering the bath. Sitting, using soap and shampoo, rinse completely. Then enter the bath.
  • No swimwear in traditional baths (some Western-style onsen hotels allow it)
  • No phones/cameras in the bathing area — ever
  • Tie long hair up so it doesn't touch the water
  • Stay in the bath 10–15 min max, then cool down before re-entering
  • Rotenburo = outdoor bath. Worth seeking out, especially with a mountain or garden view.

🛍️ Shopping

  • Tax-free: Spend ¥5,000+ at one store with passport for 10% back. Works at electronics, department stores, many pharmacies.
  • Don Quijote (Donki): Everything. 24 hrs. Snacks, electronics, costumes, beauty, souvenirs. Cheapest prices.
  • Animate (Ikebukuro): 8 floors of anime merch. No better place in Tokyo.
  • Gashapon Dept. Store (Ikebukuro): 3,000–4,000 machines, ¥300–400/capsule
  • Bic Camera / Yodobashi: Electronics, tax-free with passport
  • Daiso / Seria / Can★Do: 100-yen shops. Incredible for gifts — stationery, kitchen items, unique designs
  • Narita airport duty-free: Japanese whisky (Hibiki, Nikka, Yamazaki), matcha KitKats, Shiroi Koibito cookies — often as cheap or cheaper than city prices

🙏 Etiquette

  • No eating while walking (except festival stalls) — hold the item still or finish before moving
  • No tipping — ever. It causes confusion and mild offense.
  • Loud talking on trains = rude. Keep phones on silent. Speak quietly or not at all.
  • Bow slightly when thanking people: 15° = thank you, 30° = I'm sorry, 45° = deep apology
  • Shoes off when entering homes and many ryokan — look for the genkan (entrance step)
  • Tattoo caution: some onsen, some ryokan, and some public pools prohibit tattoos. Always check before paying.
  • Queuing: Japan takes queues very seriously. Always line up properly. Do not jump.
  • Trash cans are rare — carry a small bag for your garbage. You'll end up at a convenience store eventually.

🌧️ Rainy Day

  • June = rainy season. Plan for at least 3–4 rainy days. Embrace it.
  • Best rainy options: teamLab Borderless (fully indoor), Akihabara (covered arcades), Kappabashi Kitchen Street (covered), Nishiki Market (covered), any major museum, any Department Store (B1 food halls are incredible)
  • Temples in rain: Senso-ji, Fushimi Inari (atmospheric + fewer tourists), Arashiyama Bamboo Grove (mist is beautiful)
  • Department store basement food halls (depachika) — incredible quality, massive variety. Every department store has one.
  • Compact umbrellas: buy one at any konbini for ¥500–800 if caught in rain. Convenience stores also sell ponchos.
  • Indoor Tokyo alternatives: teamLab, Tokyo National Museum (Ueno), Mori Art Museum (Roppongi), Shinjuku/Ikebukuro covered shopping arcades
